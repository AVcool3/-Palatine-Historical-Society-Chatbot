# The George Clayson House Museum

**Source:** Compiled from public reference material via web search
(Palatine Historical Society, Palatine Park District, Wikipedia).

## The house and George Clayson

The Clayson House was built as a residence in **1873** by **George Henry
Clayson**, a nurseryman who served on the Palatine Village Board. Clayson was
born in Cuba, New York in **1833** and moved to Palatine from New York in the
1860s. In **1867** he purchased ten acres of land from Palmer Webster and
operated it as a nursery, growing fruit trees and grapes.

The house is a Victorian-era single-family home and now stands as a reminder of
Palatine's Victorian past. It is listed on the **National Register of Historic
Places**.

## Acquisition and restoration

In **1976**, in recognition of the American Bicentennial, the Palatine Public
Library bought the property from Citizens Bank & Trust, with the **Palatine
Historical Society** and the **Palatine Park District** as co-trustees.
Restoration architect **Wilbert Hasbrouck** was hired to return the house to its
original style and floor plan. From **1976 to 1986** the Society raised
**$200,000** to restore the museum.

## The museum

The Clayson House Museum **opened in October 1979** and, after several
renovations, was restored to the original single-family Victorian-era house
built in 1873. The museum holds **more than 1,000 artifacts** from Palatine's
earliest days.
