# Palatine, Illinois — Overview & Founding

**Source:** Compiled from public reference material (Wikipedia, Village of Palatine,
Palatine Historical Society) via web search. See `SOURCES.md` for links.

## Overview

Palatine is a village in northwest Cook County, Illinois, a suburb of Chicago.
As of the 2020 census, Palatine had a population of **67,908**. The median age
was 39.9 years, with 20.8% of residents under 18 and 15.4% aged 65 or older.

## Earliest settlement

The first European-American generally thought to have settled in the Palatine
area was **George Ela**, who built a log cabin in the area later called
**Deer Grove**. He was one of the first of a wave of pioneers to migrate to
northern Illinois following the Black Hawk War. Settlement began in the 1830s,
with George Ela's log cabin dated to **1835**.

Early arrivals came primarily from New York and New England, drawn to the
fertile prairie for farming. **German immigrants** joined in the 1850s,
purchasing land to establish farms and small shops, and contributing to
agricultural expansion in potatoes, wheat, corn, and cattle.

## The name "Palatine"

The township's name was adopted upon its organization in **1850**. It is
commonly said to derive either from the **Palatinate region in Germany**
(reflecting the German settlers) or from a town of the same name in New York
State from which some settlers had come.

## The railroad and platting of the village

The Illinois and Wisconsin Railroad reached the Deer Grove Trading Post area in
**1853**, and rail service reached Palatine on **June 10, 1855**. The line later
became part of the **Chicago and North Western Railway**.

**Joel Wood** surveyed and laid out the Village of Palatine in **1855**. The
railroad depot was built c. 1855 by Hiram Thurston on land donated by Joel Wood.
The village grew up around the station.

## Incorporation

The Village of Palatine was **incorporated on April 2, 1866**, and **chartered
by the State of Illinois in 1869**. By the time of incorporation it was already
a community of some size, with a Methodist church.

## Early civic development

- **1868** — a new two-story school was built on Wood Street.
- **1869** — a substantial Lutheran church was built.
- **1871** — street lights were installed after the Village purchased 12 lamps
  for $3 each and hired a lamplighter for 50 cents a night.

The "great Age of Agriculture" in Palatine Township lasted from about 1835 to
about 1950. The town grew rapidly beginning in the 1970s as part of Chicago's
suburban expansion.
